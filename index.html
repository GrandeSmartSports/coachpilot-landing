CHAPTER 01

IT STARTED WITH A BILL.

Last summer, a Bonney Lake dad signed his two daughters up for softball camps. The total came to more than a thousand dollars. He paid it, because that is what parents do. But it stuck with him.

And here is the thing: the rec leagues are not the problem. Rec programs and little leagues are the affordable heart of youth sports, and they stay that way the only way they can. They run on volunteers. Volunteers helping volunteers. It works, it matters, and he coaches in those same leagues himself. Nobody there is the bad guy. Everybody is doing their best with what they have.

The problem is the step after the league. When a kid wants more reps, more coaching, more growth than a volunteer season can give her, the options around here were camps that run into the thousands, private lessons at eighty dollars an hour, and select teams charging four figures a season. Say it plainly: that is ridiculous. Between the league and the luxury tier, there was nothing. That missing middle is where kids stop getting better, and where too many of them quietly stop playing.

"Parents should never have to choose between signing their kid up for a sport and everything else a family needs. And that is where this was headed."Coach Daniel · Founder
CHAPTER 02

SO WE BUILT THE THING THAT WAS MISSING.

That dad happens to be a coach. Twenty years of it, five sports, and a stubborn belief that the development years are the most important years in sports: they are where a kid either falls in love with the game or walks away for good.

So he formed CoachPilot LLC and started making calls. And people showed up: dedicated volunteers, experienced adults and high school players from area programs, willing to give their summer nights to these girls because they believe what he believes. The school district approved the fields. The insurance got bought. The nets got built in a garage.

Then the families showed up. More than forty girls signed up for the first clinics before a single whistle blew.

CHAPTER 03

WHAT A NIGHT WITH US ACTUALLY LOOKS LIKE

Anyone can rent a field and run lines. Ours run different, on purpose. Every session is planned to the minute, every drill scales to the kid in front of it, and every girl goes home with something to tell her mom about.

THE BIG CIRCLE

Every clinic opens with everyone in one circle, stretching while we talk: why we are here, the rules, and the promise that this field is a place to try things.

BE LOUD

Watch softball at the highest level: the dugout is on its feet start to finish. We teach field voice like a skill, because girls play better when their team is behind them.

THE BRACELET BUCKET

Do something that catches a coach's eye or ear, hustle, a gutsy call, cheering a teammate, and you earn a bracelet from the bucket. We say why, out loud, every time.

EVERY GIRL LEVELS UP

Drills run on ladders: three clean reps and you move back a cone, get a faster feed, a smaller target. Nobody is stuck in a beginner group. You climb the drill at your own speed.

SMALL GROUPS, REAL REPS

Groups of four to six, run by a coach or a high school player who lives the game. We count reps per girl per night, because standing in line is not development.

ONE WIN, EVERY NIGHT

Our closing huddle rule: every girl leaves with one named win. Her first clean catch, her loudest call, her best swing. She will remember it longer than we will.

CHAPTER 04

NEVER THE ALL-STAR.
ALWAYS THE COACH.

Daniel has been on a field or a court since he was five years old, with no break since: baseball through high school, football and wrestling from sixth grade on. He will tell you himself that he was never the all-star. He was the kid with the eye for coaching, and he knew it early. By his own high school years he was already coaching middle school wrestlers, and he spent his twenties coaching and refereeing high school wrestling.

Then his daughters were born, and everything found its place. He discovered the level of the game he loves most: the development years, rec sports, where everything is raw and everything matters. He has coached rec soccer, basketball, volleyball, and softball, and at this level he will tell you the skills transfer everywhere. But his love lives on the diamond, in the red clay. It was baseball first. Two daughters made it softball.

His coaching is simple to describe because he actually lives it: loud and positive, reps over lectures, fair as a level line. At this age everyone plays. Spots get earned later; falling in love with the game comes first.

Daniel, his wife, and their two girls moved to Bonney Lake from Arizona in the fall of 2025. If he is not on a field, he is standing in a river with a fly rod.

CHAPTER 05

WHERE THIS IS REALLY GOING

Here is the quiet truth about youth sports. At the level that matters most, your kid's season depends on whichever volunteer raised a hand: usually a parent handed a bag of balls, no training, and a good luck. Some kids win that lottery. Most don't.

We think the coaches deserve better too. The parents who give up their evenings to coach are the backbone of every league in America, and almost nobody trains them, equips them, or even says thank you. Fix the coach, and you fix the season for twelve kids at once.
